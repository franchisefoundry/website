## The honest answer: it depends on the opportunity, not the timing

There's no shortage of headlines about challenges facing UK businesses. Hospitality operators are contending with rising employment costs, business rates, energy bills and cautious consumer spending. Some established businesses are closing; others are adapting to protect margins.

Against this backdrop, anyone considering a franchise investment could reasonably ask: *Is now actually a good time?*

The truth is straightforward: it depends far more on the specific opportunity than on current market conditions.

## The operating environment is genuinely challenging

The UK hospitality sector operates under significant cost pressure. Labour, business rates and energy remain major headwinds. Consumer behaviour is shifting too—customers are increasingly conscious of value and more selective about spending.

The Autumn Budget 2026 introduced some business rates support for qualifying retail, hospitality and leisure businesses in England. From April 2027, the threshold for full Small Business Rate Relief rises to £18,000 in rateable value, with tapered relief up to £21,000. The National Living Wage will rise to £13.21 per hour from April 2027.

These changes highlight a mixed picture: some support, but continued pressure on employment and operating costs. This makes financial due diligence particularly important for prospective franchisees.

## Franchising offers advantages over starting from scratch

Starting independently means building everything yourself: brand, suppliers, procedures, staff, customer base and proving the model works commercially.

A quality franchise can provide:

• An established brand and proposition
• A proven operating model
• Existing supplier relationships
• Training and operational support
• Marketing resources and brand recognition
• Guidance on site selection and launch
• Access to an established franchisee network

These advantages reduce some uncertainty—but don't confuse an established franchise system with a guaranteed return. A franchise is still a business. Performance depends on location, costs, management, customer demand and the strength of the underlying model.

## Not every franchise can withstand difficult trading conditions

Resilience matters. Two businesses in the same sector can have vastly different financial profiles. One restaurant might require a large premises, significant staffing and substantial overheads. Another might operate from a smaller footprint with a simpler menu, lower staffing and flexible operations.

Before committing, examine:

• Break-even revenue requirements
• Staffing as a percentage of sales
• Sensitivity to food and supplier costs
• Fixed monthly overheads
• Working capital needed
• Performance when sales fall below forecast

The strongest opportunity isn't necessarily the biggest-name brand. It's the one with a commercially sustainable model that suits *you*.

## Your franchisor matters as much as the franchise

A franchise investment is an investment in the people and organisation behind the brand. Before committing, understand their experience, financial position, operational capability and support commitment.

Ask:

• How many franchised and company-owned locations operate?
• How long have existing franchisees been trading?
• What post-opening support is provided?
• How frequently does the franchisor communicate with franchisees?
• What happens when a franchisee faces difficulties?
• Can you speak with existing franchisees directly?

Also consider whether the franchisor has successfully supported businesses through challenging conditions. A franchise should offer more than a logo and manual—it should provide infrastructure, knowledge and ongoing support.

## The right opportunity depends on the right investor

There's no universally best franchise. Requirements differ significantly:

• A **hands-on owner** may suit a business requiring daily involvement
• An **experienced operator** may manage a multi-unit portfolio more effectively
• An **investor with limited sector experience** needs stronger training and support
• Someone seeking **passive investment** needs to understand whether the franchise actually allows that, and what management infrastructure is required

The mistake is choosing a franchise because the brand looks attractive, without first establishing whether the business suits *you*.

## Don't rush because a territory is available

Franchise opportunities are sometimes marketed around available territories, expansion targets or limited availability. While genuine, these should never replace proper due diligence.

Take time to:

• Review the franchise agreement
• Understand the financial model
• Assess the market
• Speak to existing franchisees
• Seek independent legal and financial advice

A good franchisor welcomes difficult questions and respects thorough due diligence.

## So, is now a good time?

Challenges exist in the UK market, but that doesn't eliminate good franchise opportunities. In fact, challenging conditions can make the quality of the underlying business model even more important.

Avoid decisions based purely on market sentiment, brand recognition or growth promises. Instead:

• Look at the numbers
• Understand the operation
• Assess the franchisor
• Be realistic about commitment required

The real question isn't whether *now* is a good time to buy a franchise. It's whether the *particular franchise* you're considering is a good investment *for you*.
