## The Campaign: 800 Businesses, One Ask

On 14 September 2026, an unprecedented coalition of more than 800 hospitality businesses—from Michelin-starred restaurants to independent pubs, Greene King to JD Wetherspoon—wrote an open letter to Prime Minister Andy Burnham. The signatories, backed by over 370,000 petition signatures, made a single, direct demand: **cut hospitality VAT from 20% to 10%**.

The timing was deliberate. Burnham had publicly backed a lower hospitality VAT rate before taking office. The coalition delivered their letter on the same day he hosted business leaders at Downing Street, pitching his government as a "partner for growth."

This is the biggest coordinated hospitality campaign in years. For anyone operating or evaluating a hospitality franchise, understanding what it means—and what it doesn't—is essential.

## Why 10%? The European Benchmark

The #VATsTheProblem campaign argues that a 10% rate would bring the UK in line with most of Europe. France, Italy, Spain, and Germany all charge reduced VAT on hospitality, typically between 7% and 10%. The UK, at 20%, applies the highest standard rate in Europe (alongside Denmark).

The case is stark:

- Three hospitality venues are closing every day
- 3,353 insolvencies in 2025
- Profits at the top 100 restaurant groups down 44%

During the pandemic, when VAT was cut to 5% then 12.5%, operators reported measurably higher revenue, stronger consumer demand, and improved viability. The evidence that lower VAT works is not theoretical—it's proven.

## The Unit Economics: What It Actually Means

### Direct Margin Impact

For a unit turning over £500,000, moving from 20% to 10% VAT could reduce the liability by approximately **£38,000 annually**.

However, this doesn't flow entirely to your bottom line. Competitive pressure will force some of the saving onto consumers. Industry estimates suggest **30–50% of the VAT saving flows directly to operating margin**.

For a unit currently running a net margin of £40,000–£50,000, a saving of £15,000–£20,000 would represent a **30–40% improvement in profitability**.

### Demand Effect

Operators who pass VAT savings to consumers through lower prices typically see improved footfall and sales. Pandemic-era data on the 5% rate showed measurable uplift in consumer spending. A 10% rate would deliver a smaller boost, but still meaningful—especially in QSR and fast-casual where price sensitivity is highest.

## How to Think About This When Evaluating a Franchise

Here's the critical bit: **the VAT cut is not confirmed**. It's a demand, not government policy.

Cutting hospitality VAT to 10% would cost the Treasury £10–12 billion annually. That requires either offsetting revenue elsewhere or a political decision to accept a significant fiscal cost. The Budget is where this will be decided.

### Build Your Model on Current Reality

**Do not** include a VAT reduction in your franchise unit economics. Building a financial case on an assumption rather than a fact is building on risk. The difference between an assumption and a fact is who bears the risk when the assumption proves wrong—and that's you.

What the campaign does change is the **probability** and the **policy context**. A Prime Minister under significant pressure to deliver on a pre-election commitment faces stronger incentives than previous governments.

### The Right Risk Profile

The franchisees best positioned to benefit are those who chose a format with sound unit economics **at current 20% VAT rates**. A business already viable at 20% becomes substantially stronger at 10%. A business that only works at 10% VAT has already failed before the policy arrives.

## What to Do Right Now

**If you're operating a franchise unit:** Review your pricing strategy to understand what a 10% VAT rate would mean for menu prices and margins. You won't implement changes now, but knowing your response in advance matters—competitors will move fast if the policy lands.

**If you're evaluating a franchise opportunity:** Build unit economics on current VAT. Model what the same unit looks like at 10% VAT as a sensitivity analysis. If the business only makes sense at 10%, that's critical information. If it works at 20% and becomes substantially stronger at 10%, you're looking at a reasonable risk profile with upside.

**If you're timing a franchise investment:** The policy environment around hospitality in the UK is more constructive than it has been in years. That matters for a five-year investment decision, even if it doesn't change the unit economics you should be using today.

## The Bottom Line

The hospitality franchise sector is navigating a genuinely difficult period. But policy direction, for the first time in several years, looks more favourable. The businesses best positioned to benefit from a VAT cut are the ones that didn't wait for policy to arrive before building a viable operation.

If the cut comes, you'll be ready. If it doesn't, you're still in business.
