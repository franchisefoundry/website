Capital is the most common practical barrier between a prospective franchisee and the opportunity they want to pursue. It's also where the most confusion exists — about what's available, what lenders actually want, and how your choice of brand affects how much you can borrow.

This is a plain-English guide to franchise funding for hospitality investments in the UK in 2026: what the options are, what the process looks like, and what you need before approaching a lender.

## The funding landscape: what has changed in 2026

The franchise lending market in the UK has matured considerably. The major high street banks — NatWest, Barclays, Lloyds, HSBC, and Metro Bank — all run dedicated franchise lending desks staffed by specialists who understand the franchise model and maintain approved lending lists.

Being on a bank's approved list gives you access to significantly better terms.

Interest rates have moved materially since the low-rate era of 2020–2022. Business lending rates in 2026 are running significantly higher, so the interest cost needs to be properly modelled into your unit economics. A loan repayment schedule built at current rates looks different to one from 2021.

On the positive side, fintech lenders — Funding Circle, iwoca, OakNorth, and others — have expanded options available to franchisees whose business models don't fit classic bank criteria. Faster applications, more flexible underwriting, and approaches suited to hospitality's seasonal cash flow patterns have made this increasingly viable.

The British Business Bank's Growth Guarantee Scheme continues to provide government-backed guarantees to commercial lenders, making capital more accessible where security is limited. Start Up Loans — unsecured personal loans up to £25,000 at 6% fixed, with free mentoring — remain useful for lower-capital entry or as a top-up alongside bank lending.

## How much can you borrow?

The answer depends on two things: the brand you're investing in, and how much of your own capital you're contributing.

For established brands with proven UK track records on bank approved lists, the major banks typically fund **50–70% of total investment** including working capital. Barclays, for example, offers up to 70% of approved costs for recognised brands, with unsecured borrowing available up to £100,000 and secured lending to £10 million.

For newer brands or concepts without an established UK track record, that ratio typically falls to **30–50%**, pushing more burden onto your own capital.

A well-established brand with strong franchisee performance data does more than reduce operational risk — it improves your access to funding and the terms available.

Most lenders expect you to contribute **at least 30% of total investment** from your own capital. They'll want evidence that those funds have been saved over time, not raised through a personal loan or remortgage. This signals personal investment in success, and lenders weight it accordingly.

## The main funding routes

**High street bank franchise lending** is the most common route for investments above £100,000. The dedicated franchise desks understand the model and have pre-approved brands they fund more efficiently.

**Critical point:** always approach the bank's franchise team directly, not your local branch manager. A local manager may lack franchise knowledge to assess your application properly, and a rejection from the wrong contact can affect subsequent applications.

Term loans typically run for the length of the initial franchise agreement — usually five years. Some lenders offer capital repayment holidays in the first three to six months while the business establishes itself.

**Start Up Loans** are useful for lower-investment entry points. Administered through the British Business Bank, they offer unsecured personal loans up to £25,000 per individual at 6% fixed, with one to five year repayment terms. They require no personal guarantee or assets as security and come with free mentoring. For a mobile coffee franchise or lower-capital QSR entry, they can provide meaningful top-up funding alongside personal capital.

**Asset finance and hire purchase** are worth considering for physical assets — commercial kitchen equipment, refrigeration, vehicles, point-of-sale systems, and fit-out. By spreading costs over asset useful life, you keep working capital in the business rather than tying it up in depreciating equipment. Many hospitality investments can separate the asset finance component from the working capital loan, improving the overall funding package.

**Fintech and alternative lenders** are increasingly relevant for hospitality franchisees needing faster decisions or whose profile doesn't fit standard bank criteria. They're typically more expensive than high street lending, but for a funding gap or seasonal cash flow requirement they may be the most efficient solution.

**Franchisor financing** is available from some brands. Certain concepts offer deferred franchise fee arrangements, in-house payment plans, or relationships with specialist franchise funders. Scrutinise these carefully — a deferred franchise fee with a high implicit interest rate is a cost, not a concession.

## What lenders want to see

**Your unit economics model.** This is the centrepiece of any funding application. The lender wants to see that you understand the full cost structure — food and labour costs as a percentage of revenue, royalty obligations, rent and rates, energy, and loan repayment — and that the model generates sufficient net margin to service the debt.

A model that only works under optimistic assumptions undermines your application. Build it from current figures — current National Living Wage, current National Insurance rates, current business rates under 2026 structure — not from a franchisor's projection prepared two years ago.

**Your personal financial position.** The lender will assess your credit history, existing liabilities, and ability to sustain an income gap during early trading. If you're leaving employment, the period before the unit generates profit needs to be funded. Show you've considered this and have a plan.

**The brand's track record.** A brand with five years of franchisee performance data and a history of franchisees meeting projections is fundamentally different lending risk to one franchising for eighteen months with a small number of sites. Your brand choice directly affects both access to lending and available terms.

**Your relevant experience.** Lenders want evidence that you can manage a team, operate under pressure, and make sound commercial decisions. Document relevant experience — management roles, customer-facing careers, financial responsibility — clearly in your business plan.

## The business plan: what to include

Every franchise funding application requires a business plan. Its quality is the primary determinant of the lending terms you receive.

A franchise business plan for hospitality should include:

- Executive summary
- Description of the franchise opportunity and brand track record
- Market analysis of your specific location
- Unit economics model built from current cost benchmarks
- Cash flow forecast covering at least three years
- Your personal financial summary
- Your relevant experience

The most common weakness is over-reliance on the franchisor's projections without independent stress-testing. A franchisor's projection illustrates the opportunity. A lender wants to see that you've interrogated those projections, adjusted them for current conditions, and modelled what happens if trading comes in below forecast in year one.

A business plan demonstrating genuine understanding of unit economics and clear evidence of independent research outperforms one that's essentially the franchisor's information pack reformatted.

## The sequence that works

**Start with unit economics.** Before approaching any lender, build the full model for your specific opportunity. Know your prime cost, royalty obligation, rent and rates liability, and net margin under realistic assumptions. This is the foundation of your business plan and the basis for every lending conversation.

**Choose a brand banks recognise.** A brand on the major banks' approved lists gives you access to better lending terms and a faster process. Ask specifically whether the brand has established lending relationships with high street franchise desks.

**Approach the franchise lending desk, not the branch.** Contact the franchise team at two or three banks directly, in parallel. Present your unit economics model and business plan. Ask what they'd need to see to approve funding and what terms they'd offer. Compare responses before committing to a lender.

**Consider the full funding structure.** The most efficient package may combine a high street bank term loan for the franchise fee and working capital, asset finance for equipment and fit-out, and personal capital for the required deposit. Getting these components right before signing anything matters — total funding cost affects your unit economics from day one.

## A word on timing

Franchise funding takes longer than most prospective franchisees expect. From initial approach to approved funding, the process typically runs four to eight weeks at a specialist lender, longer if the business plan requires reworking.

If you're working to a specific timeline — a discovery day, a franchise agreement signing date — build the funding process in early, not at the end.

The National Franchise Exhibition on 2–3 October at Olympia London is an excellent opportunity for face-to-face conversations with franchise lending specialists from the major banks. A ten-minute conversation can tell you more about what you need to prepare than weeks of online research.
