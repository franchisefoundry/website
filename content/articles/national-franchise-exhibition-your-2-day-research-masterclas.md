The National Franchise Exhibition runs 2–3 October 2026 at Olympia London. Entry is free, over 100 brands will be exhibiting, and there's a full programme of educational seminars. But here's the thing: the difference between franchisees who walk away with genuine insight and those who leave with a bag full of brochures comes down to one thing — preparation.

## What the show actually is

The exhibition is organised by Venture Marketing Group in partnership with the British Franchise Association. It's a genuine research tool, not a decision-making environment.

The brands exhibiting are paying to be there. That means they've sent people who know the business — franchise development managers, operations directors, sometimes founders and CEOs — and they're motivated to have real conversations. The seminars are run by practitioners covering topics like reading a franchise agreement and building a business plan for your lender.

What it isn't is the place to make your final call. The show floor is busy, exhibitors are competing for attention, and face-to-face conversations naturally push toward optimism. The real work of due diligence happens afterwards, in the numbers and in conversations with existing franchisees.

## Before you go: three things worth doing

**Know your format.** If you're interested in hospitality — QSR, coffee, pubs, casual dining — decide which format suits your profile before you arrive. If you've already aligned your capital, experience, and working-life ambitions with a specific concept and investment range, you can walk the floor with purpose rather than curiosity.

**Prepare your questions in advance.** Every conversation should cover the same ground:

- What is the average net margin for an established unit?
- What does bottom-quartile performance look like?
- What's the combined food and labour cost as a percentage of revenue?
- How many franchisees have exited in the past three years, and why?
- What does the royalty obligation look like in a loss-making month?

A brand that deflects these questions is giving you information too.

**Set a limit of three to five brands.** More than that and conversations blur into each other, questions become perfunctory, and you'll leave with materials you won't process. Pick brands that match your format and investment criteria before you walk in.

## On the show floor: what to do at each stand

**Ask to speak with the franchisee support team, not just sales.** Most brands will have franchise development people on the stand. What you also want is a conversation with whoever handles existing franchisee support. Support infrastructure is what matters once you've signed.

**Ask about existing franchisees directly.** Request introductions to two or three real operators — not the testimonials on the website. Ask specifically for franchisees who've been trading for fewer than two years. If the brand is reluctant to make introductions, note that.

**Take notes immediately after each conversation.** Not during — you'll miss things and the conversation will feel transactional. In the two minutes after you leave each stand, write down the three things you learned and the two things you still need to find out.

**Attend at least two seminars.** The programme covers what matters most: understanding franchise agreements, funding, what makes a successful franchisee, and how to conduct due diligence. These are educational sessions run by experts, not sales pitches. Plan which ones you'll attend before you arrive.

## The conversations worth having beyond the brand stands

The most underused part of the exhibition isn't the brand stands — it's everything else.

**Finance providers.** Barclays, NatWest, HSBC, and Lloyds all have dedicated franchise lending desks at the show. A ten-minute conversation with a franchise finance specialist will tell you more about what a lender will actually need from you than a week of online research. Ask specifically what they'd want to see in a business plan for your investment range.

**Franchise lawyers.** A good franchise solicitor isn't just someone who reviews your agreement at the end — they can tell you what to look for before you get there. Ask them what the most common mistakes prospective franchisees make. You'll get candid answers in a show environment.

**The BFA team.** The British Franchise Association's team can direct you to resources, answer questions about industry standards and accreditation, and help you understand what membership means for the brands you're evaluating.

## After the show: what to do with what you've learned

**Follow up on franchisee introductions within 48 hours.** Chase that introduction while the show context is fresh. Prepare specific questions — not general impressions, but specific unit economics data and honest assessments of what the first year looked like versus projections.

**Build or update your unit economics model.** The conversations should have given you better cost structure data. Use it. A more accurate unit economics model is the single most useful output of the show.

**Decide which brands warrant a discovery day.** A discovery day is a brand's formal next step. Don't go for any brand you're not genuinely interested in. The show should narrow your shortlist, not expand it.
