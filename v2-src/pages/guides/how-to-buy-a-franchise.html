Seven steps, in the order they actually happen. Written by someone who’s sat on the brand’s side of the table.

THE SHORT VERSION
  • Work out your real budget, including working capital, before you look at brands.
  • Decide how hands-on you want to be. It narrows the field more than sector does.
  • Check the numbers yourself and speak to existing franchisees before you sign.

1. Work out what you can really afford

The franchise fee is only part of it. Fit-out, equipment, legal fees and the cash to cover the first months of trading all come before you see a return. Most lenders also want to see some of your own money in the deal.

[Rule-of-thumb guidance on deposit and working capital, written by Ben.]

2. Decide how you want to run it

Hands-on, manager-run, or hands-on for a year then step back. Some brands insist the owner is on the floor; others are built for a manager from day one. Be honest about this early and you’ll save weeks.

3. Shortlist on fit, not logos

A brand you love as a customer isn’t automatically a good business for you. Compare on budget, territory, hours and growth path first. That’s why we hide brand names until the call.

4. Check the numbers yourself

Ask for real P&Ls from sites like the one you’d open, not the best performer. Then call existing franchisees, ideally ones the brand didn’t pick for you.

WORTH ASKING
“How many sites have closed or changed hands in the last three years, and why?”

5. Make the most of discovery day

It’s an interview both ways. Go with your questions written down, watch how the support team talks about current franchisees, and leave knowing exactly what happens in your first 90 days.

6. Sort your funding

High-street banks with franchise teams, specialist lenders and government-backed schemes are the usual routes. A brand’s track record makes lenders more comfortable, so bring their numbers with you.

7. Get the agreement checked, then sign

Use a solicitor who specialises in franchising. Focus on term, renewal, territory, exit and what happens if things go wrong. Don’t sign anything with a deadline you didn’t agree to.
